**JEPI (the JPMorgan Equity Premium Income ETF) pays dividends every month**, usually with the ex-dividend date near the end of the month and the payment in the first week of the following month. Unlike a regular dividend stock, JEPI's monthly distribution is **not fixed** — it changes every month based on the income from the fund's covered-call strategy.

## JEPI dividend calendar: monthly schedule

JEPI distributes twelve times a year. The exact ex-dividend and payment dates shift slightly each month and are only final once JPMorgan declares them.

| Month | Typical distribution timing |
|-------|----------------------------|
| Jan | Early January |
| Feb | Early February |
| Mar | Early March |
| Apr | Early April |
| May | Early May |
| Jun | Early June |
| Jul | Early July |
| Aug | Early August |
| Sep | Early September |
| Oct | Early October |
| Nov | Early November |
| Dec | Early December |

The ex-dividend date usually falls in the last few days of the prior month, with payment following a few days later. Recent payments have landed around the first few days of each month, but treat this as a pattern, not a fixed calendar.

## How the dividend cycle works

- **Declaration date:** JPMorgan announces the amount and the key dates for the month.
- **Ex-dividend date:** the cutoff. Buy before this date to receive the distribution; buy on or after it and the seller keeps it.
- **Record date:** usually one business day after the ex-dividend date.
- **Payment date:** the cash lands in your brokerage account, typically within a few business days of the ex-dividend date.

For JEPI, the date that matters for your trading is the **ex-dividend date**.

## How much does JEPI pay?

JEPI's monthly distribution changes every month because it is generated by selling call options (through Equity-Linked Notes) on the S&P 500, not by a fixed company payout. Over recent months the per-share distribution has been roughly **$0.37 to $0.39**, but that is a recent range, not a guarantee — in high-volatility months the distribution can be larger, and in quiet markets it shrinks. The trailing yield has been in the **7% to 10%** area, but it moves with the share price and the option income. Model the income with the [monthly dividend calculator](/calculators/monthly-dividend-calculator).

## Why JEPI's distribution changes every month

JEPI is a covered-call fund. It holds the S&P 500 and sells call options on it, and the premium from those options is what boosts the distribution above an ordinary dividend yield. When markets are volatile, option premiums rise and the distribution tends to be larger; when markets are calm and rising, premiums fall and the distribution shrinks, and the call options can also cap JEPI's upside. Part of the distribution may be classified as return of capital in some periods. This is different from a stock like SCHD, which pays a quarterly dividend from portfolio income — see the [SCHD dividend calendar](/blog/schd-dividend-calendar) for that cadence.

## How to confirm the exact 2026 dates

1. Open the official JPMorgan fund page for JEPI and check the &ldquo;Distributions&rdquo; section.
2. Watch for the declaration announcement in the days before each ex-dividend date.
3. In your brokerage, the ex-dividend and payment dates appear under the corporate-action details for your position.

Dates published before the official declaration are estimates. Always confirm the declared dates before trading around a distribution.

*Dividend dates and amounts can change and are not official until declared. JEPI distributions are variable and may include return of capital. This page is information, not financial or tax advice.*
