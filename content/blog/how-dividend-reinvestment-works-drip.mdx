title: "How Dividend Reinvestment (DRIP) Works"

description: "A DRIP reinvests your dividends into more shares, so next year pays dividends on more shares. See worked compounding math, fractional shares, and tax rules."
---

<p>A <strong>DRIP</strong> (Dividend Reinvestment Plan) automatically uses every cash dividend payout to buy additional full or fractional shares of the same stock or ETF. Instead of receiving cash in your brokerage sweep account, your share count rises on every payout date — and each future dividend is calculated on that larger share count.</p>

<h2>The compounding mechanics (worked example)</h2>

<p>Suppose you invest <strong>$10,000</strong> into a dividend-growth stock trading at <strong>$100 per share</strong> (100 shares) paying a <strong>$4.00 annual dividend</strong> (4.0% yield), and the company increases its dividend by <strong>6% per year</strong> while share price grows 5% per year:</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Year 1:</strong> Your 100 shares generate $400 in dividends. Reinvested automatically at ~$105/share, you add 3.81 shares and enter Year 2 with <strong>103.81 shares</strong>.</li>
  <li><strong>Year 5:</strong> Dividend increases plus reinvested shares lift annual dividend income to roughly <strong>$590/year</strong> without adding a dollar of new cash.</li>
  <li><strong>Year 15:</strong> Your position holds ~176 shares paying ~$9.04/share — roughly <strong>$1,590/year</strong> in annual dividends on the original $10,000 cost basis.</li>
</ul>

<p>The spread widens sharply in years 10–20 because reinvested dividends accumulate shares faster whenever share prices dip or trade sideways. Buying during flat markets locks in more shares per dividend dollar, accelerating future income once payouts step up again.</p>

<h2>Why dividend growth beats chasing extreme yield</h2>

<p>A 3% starting yield growing at 7% annually overtakes a flat 6% yield within roughly 15 years on reinvested income — and typically carries far lower balance-sheet risk. When a stock yields 10%+ with minimal growth, the market is often pricing in dividend stagnation or an eventual cut, which breaks compounding immediately.</p>

<h2>Key rules and tax notes before enabling DRIP</h2>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Taxes still apply in taxable accounts:</strong> In the US and most jurisdictions, reinvested dividends are taxed in the year paid just like cash distributions. Keep cost-basis lots tracked (most brokers track DRIP lots automatically) or hold high-yield positions in tax-advantaged accounts like an IRA/Roth IRA.</li>
  <li><strong>Use realistic assumptions:</strong> Model 3–6% dividend growth rather than double-digit extrapolations unless payout ratios and free cash flow clearly support it.</li>
  <li><strong>Zero-commission fractional execution:</strong> Nearly all major US brokerages execute DRIP purchases commission-free into fractional shares, so 100% of every payout goes to work immediately.</li>
</ul>
