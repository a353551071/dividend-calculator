**QQQI pays dividends every month — twelve times a year.** The ex-dividend date has recently landed in the **mid-to-late part of the month**, with the payment following within about a week. Unlike a regular dividend stock, QQQI's distribution is **not fixed** — it changes each month with the income from the fund's options-based strategy.

## How the dividend cycle works

- **Declaration date:** NEOS announces the amount and the key dates for the month.
- **Ex-dividend date:** the cutoff. Buy before this date to receive the distribution; buy on or after it and the seller keeps it.
- **Record date:** usually one business day after the ex-dividend date.
- **Payment date:** the cash lands in your brokerage account, typically within about a week of the ex-dividend date.

For QQQI, the date that matters for your trading is the **ex-dividend date**.

## How much does QQQI pay?

QQQI's monthly distribution changes every month because it is driven by index-option premiums on Nasdaq-100 exposure, not by a fixed company payout. Over recent months the per-share distribution has been roughly **$0.63 to $0.66**, and the **trailing 12-month distribution yield has been around 13% to 15%** — far above an ordinary Nasdaq-100 holding, because you are being paid for selling away part of the upside. ## Why QQQI's distribution is so high

QQQI holds Nasdaq-100 exposure and overlays index options to harvest premium. When volatility rises, option premiums rise and the distribution tends to be larger; in calm markets it shrinks. Two things follow from that design: part of the fund's upside is capped relative to QQQ, and some distributions may be classified as **return of capital** in certain periods. This is very different from a fund like SCHD, which pays a quarterly dividend from portfolio income — see the [SCHD dividend calendar](/blog/schd-dividend-calendar) — or JPMorgan's S&P 500 sibling JEPI on the [JEPI dividend calendar](/blog/jepi-dividend-calendar).

## How to confirm the exact 2026 dates

1. Open the official NEOS fund page for QQQI and check the &ldquo;Distributions&rdquo; section.
2. Watch for the declaration announcement in the days before each ex-dividend date.
3. In your brokerage, the ex-dividend and payment dates appear under the corporate-action details for your position.

Dates published before the official declaration are estimates. Always confirm the declared dates before trading around a distribution.
